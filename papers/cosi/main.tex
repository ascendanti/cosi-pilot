\begin{abstract}
We propose an operator-theoretic framing of the mechanistic
interpretability literature's empirical regularity that sparse
autoencoders recover monosemantic feature dictionaries from trained
transformers: namely, that these features are concrete realizations of
approximately invariant subspaces of the nonlinear forward operator,
in the sense the classical theory of invariant subspaces studies in
the linear setting. From the framing we derive a research program,
\emph{Cross-Operator Subspace Isomorphism} (COSI), whose strong claim
is that compositional reasoning subspaces are isomorphic across
sufficiently different architectures. We do \emph{not} test the strong
claim in this paper. We report a representational-alignment pilot:
two pre-registered runs in which Procrustes alignment of PCA-projected
last-token activation matrices, between architecturally different
frontier transformers on matched compositional-evaluation prompts,
produces residuals far below a row-permutation null
(empirical $p < 0.001$ in every pre-registered cell). The pilot
demonstrates that the infrastructure detects a real cross-model
representational signal, and we report it in full. The pilot does not
demonstrate that the aligned subspaces are operator-invariant, that
the alignment isolates compositional structure from lexical or
template structure, that it is robust to held-out prompts, that it
exceeds simple lexical baselines, or that it survives the
training-distribution confound common to all current frontier models.
We name each gap explicitly, specify the controls and interventions
that would close it (invariance-leakage measurement, lexical and
template baselines, train/test Procrustes, CKA cross-validation,
training-distribution-disjoint controls, causal cross-model
intervention), and run first-pass versions of four of them on the
pilot data. Linear CKA confirms strong global representational
similarity ($0.84$--$0.96$). Train/test Procrustes splits show no
overfitting. Invariance-leakage measurement shows the PCA subspaces
are partially invariant under the forward operator (more invariant
than random subspaces; absolute leakage $0.51$--$0.89$). \textbf{The
lexical-baseline study materially weakens the operator-theoretic
interpretation: the LLM--LLM Procrustes residual ($0.77$) is
indistinguishable from one of the LLMs aligned against a TF-IDF
representation of the same prompts ($0.76$), and is \emph{worse} than
a TF-IDF vs.\ template-only alignment ($0.61$).} The pilot's
representational signal is real but does not exceed what shared
lexical/template structure alone would produce. We present the
operator-theoretic program as a research agenda the pilot motivates
rather than a finding the pilot confirms. The contribution we offer
is the bridge between mechanistic interpretability and operator theory,
the operationalization of one consequence of the Platonic
Representation Hypothesis, and an honest accounting of what the
present evidence does and does not support.
\end{abstract}
